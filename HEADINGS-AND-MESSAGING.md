# Headings and Messaging — Version 4

## Core public identity

**Learning and optimization for reliable networked intelligence.**

This phrase is broad enough to include reinforcement learning, online optimization, bandits, networks, distributed AI, security, quantum networking, and autonomous systems, while remaining specific about the intellectual goal.

## Research-page identity

**Mathematical foundations for reliable networked intelligence.**

The site organizes the portfolio around five substantive questions rather than around webpage mechanics:

1. Can learning remain safe and certifiable while knowledge is incomplete?
2. When is adaptation worth its switching, ramp, communication, or reconfiguration cost?
3. What state, preference, or shared experience is worth acquiring?
4. How can local agents and cloud–edge–device tiers coordinate at scale?
5. How can decisions remain useful under drift, adversaries, stale models, and evolving threats?

## Page-level headings

- Homepage: **Learning and optimization for reliable networked intelligence.**
- Research: **Mathematical foundations for reliable networked intelligence.**
- Mentoring/teaching/outreach: **Teach mathematics as a language for intelligent systems.**
- Service: **Leadership and service for rigorous research communities.**
- Publications: **Learning, optimization, and networked intelligence.**

## Heading rule for future edits

A heading should name at least one of the following:

- a research problem;
- a technical mechanism;
- a guarantee or limitation;
- a system/application consequence;
- an educational or service contribution.

Avoid headings that merely describe page organization, such as “three mutually reinforcing thrusts,” “our framework,” or “more about this work.”

## Tone

Use precise, evidence-grounded language. Distinguish among:

- published/accepted work;
- submitted manuscripts;
- active research directions;
- outreach completed;
- outreach in development.
