How I mentorFrom a consequential question to an independent research identity
  1. Start from the decision consequence. Clarify what matters operationally, who is affected, and why a mathematical abstraction is needed.
  2. Model the essential difficulty. Separate assumptions, information, constraints, and performance goals before adding complexity.
  3. Prove, test, and diagnose. Use guarantees, lower bounds, numerical experiments, and systems evidence to understand both success and failure.
  4. Communicate across audiences. Develop papers, talks, visual explanations, proposal narratives, and educational demonstrations.

How learning progressesVisualize → formalize → analyze → implement → communicate
  1. Visualize: use small examples, diagrams, and interactive demonstrations to make abstract objects concrete.
  2. Formalize: identify variables, information, objectives, constraints, assumptions, and a performance metric.
  3. Analyze: build proof intuition, derive guarantees, examine counterexamples, and understand fundamental limits.
  4. Implement: test algorithms, compare baselines, inspect failure modes, and connect theory to systems.
  5. Communicate: explain results to classmates, researchers, engineers, teachers, and the public.
