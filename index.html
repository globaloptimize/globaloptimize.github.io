01

Learning safely at every step

Many learning algorithms limit average risk. My work studies instantaneous hard constraints, where an unsafe action or transition cannot be offset by safer behavior later.

We develop near-optimal safe reinforcement learning and extend certification to non-convex decision spaces and coupled agents, with safety guarantees that hold with high probability under the models’ stated assumptions.

Key resultsSafe exploration with mathematical guarantees

ICML 2023 & 2025 · NeurIPS 2026 accepted

Near-optimal safe RL · ICML 2023 →Non-convex safety · ICML 2025 →Coupled-agent safety · NeurIPS 2026 →
02

Adapting when change has a cost

Changing a model, route, or resource allocation consumes time and resources. I develop online optimization and learning methods that account for switching costs, ramp limits, and uncertain forecasts.

In our adversarial bandit model with switching costs, choosing two arms at a time changes regret scaling from Θ(T2/3) to O(√T), without full feedback—a precise gain from a small increase in choice.

Key resultsThe value of prediction, feedback, and flexibility

IEEE/ACM ToN · INFOCOM · ICLR · WiOpt

03

Understanding what information makes learning possible

Intelligent systems rarely observe the full state of the world. My work asks when partial online state information changes what a learning algorithm can achieve.

We establish worst-case limits and identify structured classes of partially observable problems that admit efficient learning, clarifying when additional observations are worth acquiring.

Key resultRegret bounds and limits under partial observability

IEEE Transactions on Information Theory, 2026

04

Keeping edge intelligence useful, not just up to date

A newer model is not always worth downloading. Our Age of Intelligence framework measures how model staleness affects a device’s decisions, then balances that loss against refresh costs.

Across 20 validation traces in the paper’s numerical study, parameter-aware synchronization reduced mean joint cost by 6.15% relative to the Instant-Param baseline.

Key resultDecision-aware model synchronization

AIoT 2026 · Accepted · Hui Wan, first author

Research vision

Intelligence that stays reliable
as the world changes.

My long-term goal is a theory of networked intelligence that treats safety, information, and adaptation as one decision problem. The central question: how can systems learn and coordinate when observations are incomplete, actions interact, and every update has a cost?
