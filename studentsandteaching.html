Mentoring philosophy

From a consequential question to an independent research identity.

01

Start from the decision consequence

Clarify what matters operationally, who is affected, and why a mathematical abstraction is needed.

02

Build the simplest model that preserves the difficulty

Separate assumptions, information structure, constraints, and performance goals before adding complexity.

03

Prove, test, and diagnose

Use theory, lower bounds, numerical experiments, and systems evidence to understand when a method works and why it fails.

04

Communicate for different audiences

Develop technical papers, proposal narratives, research talks, visual explanations, and educational demonstrations.

Learning design

A pathway from mathematical foundations to research independence.

1

Visualize

Use one-, two-, and three-dimensional examples, diagrams, and interactive demos to make abstract objects concrete.

2

Formalize

Identify variables, information, objectives, constraints, assumptions, and the right performance metric.

3

Analyze

Build proof intuition, derive guarantees, examine counterexamples, and understand fundamental limits.

4

Implement

Test algorithms numerically, compare baselines, inspect failure modes, and connect theory to systems.

5

Communicate

Explain the result to classmates, researchers, engineers, teachers, and the public.
